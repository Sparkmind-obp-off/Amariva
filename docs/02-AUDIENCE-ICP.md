# AMARIVA — AUDIENCE & ICP

## 1. Initial ICP Strategy

AMARIVA should not target “everyone who uses business tools”.

The initial go-to-market audience should be defined by **problem + behavior**, not demographics alone.

Prioritize people who:

- already spend time doing repetitive business/admin work,
- actively search for ways to make that work easier,
- can adopt a tool without a procurement department,
- have an identifiable financial or time cost,
- can become repeat users.

Examples of initial segments:

- independent operators,
- small business owners,
- solo professionals,
- micro teams,
- creators selling products/services,
- local operators with operational admin.

## 2. Demand Signals

High-value signals include:

- explicit “how do I…” queries,
- calculator/spreadsheet searches,
- requests for templates,
- pricing questions,
- comparison searches,
- “free tool” searches,
- recurring workflow complaints,
- comments asking for a ready-made solution,
- repeated manual work.

Prioritize **high intent** over high audience size.

## 3. Persona Layers

### Explorer
Knows the problem, looking for an answer.

AMARIVA response:
**Free content + free tool.**

### Evaluator
Has tried manual methods or competing tools.

AMARIVA response:
**Tool + comparison + proof + product.**

### Buyer
Wants a complete solution.

AMARIVA response:
**Paid product / software / solution.**

### Operator
Already uses AMARIVA and has a second problem.

AMARIVA response:
**Cross-sell + workflow expansion + recurring product.**

## 4. JTBD Framework

For every product, write:

> When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [desired outcome].

Example:

> When I need to make a quick business decision, I want a simple calculator and explanation, so I can act without building a spreadsheet from scratch.

## 5. Audience Qualification

A problem is attractive when it scores well across:

**Frequency × Pain × Intent × Ability to Pay × Ease of Demonstration × Distribution Potential**

If a problem is painful but nobody searches for it, acquisition may be hard.

If a problem is searched heavily but has little monetizable value, it may work better as a free acquisition asset.

## 6. Research Sources

Use a portfolio of signals:

- search queries,
- autocomplete,
- search-result patterns,
- community discussions,
- social conversations,
- comments,
- product reviews,
- competitor FAQs,
- support complaints,
- internal product usage.

The goal is not to “guess trends”. It is to observe repeated demand.
