# AMARIVA — RETENTION & EXPANSION

## Principle

The first sale is not the end of the funnel.

A healthy AMARIVA customer journey is:

**solve one problem → trust AMARIVA → discover adjacent problem → adopt next product**

## Retention Types

### Product Retention
The same tool/product continues to be used.

### Portfolio Retention
The customer returns to another AMARIVA product.

### Relationship Retention
The customer remains subscribed or subscribed to updates.

## Post-Purchase Sequence

### Immediately
Deliver access/value.

### Early
Help the buyer achieve first success.

### Later
Show one useful adjacent workflow.

### Ongoing
Publish improvements, new tools, and relevant education.

Avoid constant promotional email.

## Activation

Define one clear first-value event per product.

Example:

**Tool SaaS:** first completed workflow.

**Template pack:** first usable output.

**Guide:** first implemented action.

## Expansion Triggers

Cross-sell based on behavior.

Examples:

- used pricing tool → pricing template,
- used capacity tool → planning product,
- bought template → workflow software,
- active SaaS → advanced feature/plan.

## Churn Signals

Monitor:

- declining usage,
- repeated failure,
- support issues,
- no activation,
- refunds,
- cancelled subscription,
- long inactivity.

The objective is not to manipulate retention; it is to identify where value was not delivered.

## Referral

Ask for referral when the user has achieved value.

Good referral moment:

> “This solved my problem.”

Bad referral moment:

> immediately after signup.

## Expansion Metrics

- repeat purchase rate,
- product adoption breadth,
- subscription retention,
- expansion revenue,
- referral rate,
- support burden.

## Retention Rule

A retention tactic is acceptable only when it makes the customer more successful, not merely harder to leave.
